// visual language

The light theme.

Not a second system — the same one, in daylight. Type, spacing, radius and motion are inherited from the dark theme untouched. Only the palette, the elevation, and the way the lime expresses itself change.

◐ dark ○ light ↗ home in light

01 / the continuity move

Swap the two warm tones, don't invert.

The brand already owns two warm tones: a near-black and a cream. Dark mode uses the cream as ink on the near-black paper. Light mode simply swaps their roles — the cream becomes the paper, the near-black becomes the ink. Same pigments, reversed. That is what makes the two modes read as one brand under different light, instead of two designs.

Why cream, not white. A pure-white light mode would feel clinical and cold — a different brand. The warmth (#f4f1e8, a soft bone) is the through-line that ties it to the dark theme's warmth.

03 / the lime, split by job

One accent can't do both jobs on paper.

On near-black, #c4e86b works as both text and fill. On cream it's invisible as text. So in light it splits: a deep lime for anything that must read, and the bright original kept only where it still sings — selection and small fills.

04 / elevation

Soft, warm shadows — never the dark-mode slab.

Structure is carried by the hairline borders in both modes. Shadow is only for things that genuinely float, and on paper it's soft and warm-tinted (rgba(70,58,28,.14)) — a hard black shadow on cream looks cheap.

05 / type — unchanged

Same voice, both modes.

Space Grotesk + JetBrains Mono, identical sizes, weights and leading. Fonts are the brand's voice; they don't change with the lighting — changing them would make it two brands.

// the scale, on paper
display · Space Grotesk 600
lead · the calm, confident first read
mono · labels · data · 0→1

One honest caveat: dark text on light optically reads heavier than light-on-dark, so the 600 weights feel a touch bolder here. It's within tolerance; if it ever reads heavy, drop display weight to ~550 in light only.
