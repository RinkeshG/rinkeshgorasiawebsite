Companies

Savior
2017 – 2020 Acquired by Zocdoc

Founder & Product

Hospitals don't need more software. They need software that fits how they actually work.

I spent months inside hospitals before writing a single line of spec. Not observing from the outside — actually on the floor, learning how care runs versus how it reads on paper. That gap became the product.

Built operations and preventive-care software used daily across hospital teams. Owned the full lifecycle from discovery to adoption. The hardest part wasn't the technology — it was getting nurses and admin staff to trust software enough to actually use it.

Grew to 85+ hospitals and approximately $1M ARR. Acquired by Zocdoc in 2020.

2020 – 2023 Shut down

Founder & Product

The skills that actually shape your career aren't taught anywhere.

Started as a WhatsApp group. 10,000 users in six months, mostly word of mouth. Launched a mobile learning app with 100k+ watch hours, grew the community to 3,500 active members, run by a 3-person team.

Pivoted to live cohorts. Maintained a 40% completion rate (industry average: 5–10%). Generated ₹15L in revenue. Shut it down in 2023 when the unit economics didn't work at scale. I learned more from this one than from Savior.

The Graveyard

Products and experiments that didn't survive contact with reality. Documented here because the failures are often more instructive than the wins.

Hypersync
Experiment 2024 Never launched
Hypothesis Developers spend too much time manually syncing environment variables across microservices. A CLI tool could handle this securely via end-to-end encryption.
Execution Built a Rust-based CLI and a basic web dashboard in 3 weeks. Got it working perfectly for my own local environment.
Reality Tried to onboard three engineer friends. Two couldn't install the CLI due to pathing issues on Windows. The third said, "I just use Doppler for this." I had built a feature, not a product. Killed it before ever launching.
Always validate with people outside your own head before you build anything. A single honest conversation would have saved three weeks.
MicroMentor
Side project 2019 Failed to monetize
Hypothesis Junior designers want 15-minute, highly tactical feedback sessions on specific Figma files — not months-long mentorships.
Execution Spun up a marketplace. Manually recruited 10 senior designers. Hit 100 sessions booked in the first month — incredible early traction.
Reality Junior designers were willing to pay $15 for feedback. Senior designers wouldn't get out of bed for less than $100/hr. Total pricing mismatch on both sides. Shut it down rather than trying to force the unit economics to work.
Unit economics need to work from day one on both sides of a marketplace. Traction that can't survive its own pricing model is not real traction.
Upskill India
Pre-product 2019 Pivoted
Hypothesis Working professionals in India want accessible, affordable skill-building — and a community around it.
Execution Grew to 10,000 users in 6 months via WhatsApp groups and content. No product yet — pure community.
Reality The community wanted more structure than WhatsApp could offer. Pivoted the whole thing into Career Leap — so technically this one survived, just in a different form.
Community is the product. The real thing I was building was trust and belonging — the platform was almost irrelevant. Career Leap carried that forward.
