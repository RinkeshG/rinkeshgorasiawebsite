// visual language & design system · v1

The brand, made into a system you can build with.

Every rule below is derived, not chosen. Each one traces back to one of two things: a personality trait or the point of view. If a decision can't be traced, it's marked ⚠ flagged and justified honestly — or removed.

personality · engineered candor × warm craftsman pov · build in the open, stay human audience · scan & deep-read, equally latitude · refine & codify

00 / foundation

The lens everything is derived through

Two personalities held at once, and a two-part belief. The whole system is the tension between them: precise but warm, public but human. Where they pull in different directions, the audience rule breaks the tie — serve the 20-second credibility scan and the deep read.

● Precise
Systems-minded. Things line up, snap to a grid, use tabular numbers. Nothing is approximate.
drives → the grid, tabular stats, one type scale, hairline structure
● Candid
Plain-spoken. Shows the work and the receipts, including the failures. No hype, no inflation.
drives → // system labels, proof-forward layout, honest borders over fake depth
● Warm
Human, generous. Warm-black, warm off-white — never clinical blue-grey. The cat, the coffee, the wave.
drives → warm neutrals, soft radii, the rationed flourish
● Crafted
Care over flash. Restraint reads as quality. One good move beats five decorative ones.
drives → 3-tier radius, single accent, shadow reserved for what truly floats
● Unhurried
Calm confidence. Motion is reveal, not performance. The room is quiet; the signal stands out.
drives → one easing, reveal-once, lime used sparingly
THE POV — what the site argues

Build in the open: ship in public, show the thinking and the failures, never the hype. And stay human doing it — competence and warmth are the same brand, not two modes. The site is proof of a person who makes real things and tells the truth about them.

THE TIE-BREAKER — audience

A hiring manager must get to the receipts in seconds; a founder must be rewarded for reading deeper. So: data is dense and scannable, human moments breathe. Neither audience wins by default — density decides case by case.

01 / audit

Where the current site is wrong — and why

The bones are right: a real two-font system, accent-as-signal, tabular metrics, reduced-motion support. The problem is that the system was never codified, so every component invented its own values. Drift isn't a taste issue here — it directly violates Precise and reads as un-Crafted. Findings below are from the live stylesheet.

No type scale~9 body sizes: 1.07 / 1.06 / 1.02 / 1.0 / 0.97 / 0.96 / 0.95 / 0.92 / 0.90rem
Nine near-identical sizes do one job (reading text), separated by 0.01–0.02rem — differences too small to read as hierarchy, large enough to read as sloppiness. Violates Precise.fix → collapse to one 7-step scale; body = 1.0625rem everywhere
No radius system9 radii: 5 / 7 / 8 / 9 / 10 / 11 / 12 / 14 / 16px
Same-tier elements disagree: the CTA is 7px, a screenshot frame 9px, the stat grid 11px, a card 12px, another 14px. No size→radius logic. Violates Crafted.fix → 3 tiers: 8 / 12 / 16, by element size
Section rhythm driftssection tops at 44 / 52 / 54 / 56px
Four different gaps for the same job (separating sections). The eye can't find a rhythm. Plus off-grid padding everywhere: 9 / 11 / 13 / 17 / 19 / 22 / 26px. Violates Precise.fix → one section gap (64); snap all spacing to 4/8
Line-heights wander1.74 / 1.66 / 1.65 / 1.62 / 1.60 / 1.55 / 1.50
Seven leadings across body-sized text. Reading text should have one leading so paragraphs feel like one hand set them.fix → body 1.65, lead 1.6, one value per tier
Sub-legible labels0.58rem (≈9px) on screenshot chrome
Below any legible floor — fails the scan audience and reads as an afterthought, not Candid proof. No type should go under 11px.fix → micro floor = 0.6875rem (11px)
Surfaces hardcoded#15150f · #16140f · #19190f · #1b1b11 — ad hoc
Four one-off warm-darks stand in for an elevation system. Indistinguishable by eye, impossible to maintain. Un-Crafted.fix → 2 tokens: surface-1 (raised), surface-2 (inset)
Entity names disagreecompany in a row = 1.0rem · in a card = 1.22rem · in work = 1.5rem
The same kind of thing (a company name) takes three sizes across three surfaces. Hierarchy should follow role, not which component you happened to land in.fix → name = subhead (1.25) in lists, heading (1.5) only as a page-level subject
What's already right — keep it: the sans/mono split is meaningful (not decorative), accent is used as a single signal, stats are tabular, motion respects prefers-reduced-motion, and selection/focus are styled. The job is codification, not reinvention.

02 / typography

Two voices: the person, and the system

The site keeps two typefaces because the brand is two things at once. This is the single most load-bearing rule in the document.

Space Grotesk

A grotesque with character — the angled terminals and that distinctive single-storey a keep it from feeling machined. The human voice. Everything Rinkesh actually says.

precise geometry with warm quirks — the two personalities in one face
JetBrains Mono

A coding typeface. Tabular, even, structural. The system voice. Labels, metadata, timestamps, receipts — the machine annotating the human.

warm craftsman — every neutral is warm-shifted (the inks are bone, not white; the darks are coffee, not slate). A blue-grey palette would read clinical; this one reads human.

The boring states

disabled
Text drops to mut at 0.5 opacity, border line, no accent, cursor:not-allowed. Never greyed to invisibility — still legible, just clearly off.
border — default
line for hairlines and in-flow card edges. The honest default — structure you can see, no shadow.
border — interactive
line-2 for inputs, buttons, and surfaces that lift. A slightly brighter edge says "touch me".
focus
2px accent ring, 3px offset. Keyboard focus is a first-class state, not an afterthought — accessible by default.
hover — surface
Background tints to accent @ 4%; interactive borders warm toward accent 30–40%. Felt, not loud.
overlay / scrim
rgba(8,7,5,.74) + 3px blur. Dims the room but keeps it warm — never a cold black wash.
selection
accent background, #111 text. The signal colour, applied to the thing you grabbed.
positive / success
There is no separate green — accent is "yes". Success and signal are the same idea.
candid — the boring states aren't an afterthought. Disabled stays legible, focus is visible, errors get a real colour. Honesty includes the edge cases.

04 / shape & surface

Soft-but-precise corners; borders before shadows

Not pill-round (too soft, too consumer), not sharp (too cold). A small, three-tier radius family — precise, with just enough softness to read as human. Depth is honest: structure is drawn with borders; shadow is spent only where something genuinely floats.

precise + crafted — radius is a function of size, not mood. Nest by subtracting padding; never mix tiers at one level. Replaces today's nine arbitrary radii.
candor — full round is reserved for the pulse, so a circle always means "live". Shape carries meaning too.

Borders define, shadow lifts

In-flow card — border only
Sits in the page. 1px line-2, r2, no shadow. Flat and honest — depth you can't fake.
default · 99% of surfaces
Floating element — shadow earned
Genuinely above the page: the portrait, modals, the deck. Warm-black shadow, large and soft — never grey.
rare · only what truly floats
engineered candor — borders are structure you can see; a drop-shadow on a flat card is fake depth. Shadow is a budget you spend only on things that have actually left the surface.

The one card treatment

Surface surface-1 or transparent-on-bg · 1px line-2 · radius r2 · padding 24 (20 mobile) · no shadow. Hover (only if the whole card is a link): border warms to accent, background tints accent-4%, lift translateY(-2px). One treatment, every card — track record, projects, writing, all the same anatomy.

06 / motion & feedback

Calm by default, alive on contact

Four rules. Motion is reveal and response, never performance. One easing across the whole site: cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1).

1
Reveal once, calmly.
Content fades in and rises 12px as it enters — once, ~0.5s, the house easing. No loops, no bounce, no parallax. Always honour prefers-reduced-motion.
→ unhurried · restraint reads as confidence
2
Hover is instant and physical.
~0.15–0.2s: borders warm to accent, arrows nudge, tints appear. Quick and tactile — the page is awake under your cursor.
→ warm craftsman · responsive, alive, crafted
3
Confirm in place, no ceremony.
State changes — copy email, sent, saved — confirm with a small inline change or a toast (~2.6s). Never a modal for a small success.
→ build-in-the-open · low ceremony, just tell me
4
One flourish per surface, rationed.
The wave, the live clock, the deck deal — delight is deliberate and rare. Calm is the default so the one playful move actually lands.
→ crafted + stayed-human · delight you can budget

07 / voice in ui

Lowercase, first-person, no hype

Rinkesh speaks in lowercase Space Grotesk; the system speaks in terse mono labels. Buttons are honest verbs. Errors own the failure. Numbers are stated plainly with context, never inflated.

✕ off-brand
"Get Started" · "Learn More" · "Submit"
"No data available."
"Error 422: request failed."
"Revolutionary AI-powered platform!"
"We help you find your next role."
✓ on-brand
"let's build something together" · "full story →" · "copy email"
"nothing here yet — still building."
"that didn't send. try again — or just email me."
"built solo, with AI. here's what it actually does."
"i build products, usually from scratch."
candid — buttons say what happens; errors tell the truth and offer a way out; numbers carry context ("40% completion vs a 5–10% norm") instead of bragging.
warm + stayed-human — first person, lowercase, a little dry humour. It's "i", never "we". No exclamation marks, no Title Case, no marketing verbs.

The one move you can't unsee

A memorable product has one or two ownable visual moves. Three candidates — then the argument for one.

★ the signature

The off-the-clock deck
The shelf as a dealable deck of collectible cards — the human side made into a game you play, not a list you read.

The argument for "the annotated page". A signature has to be a system, not a moment — something that appears on every screen and that you can't unsee. The live clock is the best moment (keep it — it's the hero instance of the system) and the deck is a delightful flourish (rule 4 territory, one page). But only the mono annotation language is connective tissue: it's on the home page, the work page, the writing, the footer, every label and every link.

It's also the most defensibly ownable, because it is the brand in one move: the human voice (warm craftsman) and the system voice (engineered candor) sharing every screen, with the machine quietly showing the receipts — build in the open. Cheap to apply, impossible to fake, and it says the whole foundation at a glance.
