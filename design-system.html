The Ledger.

The site is a document, not a screen: an operator's record, warmly typeset and calm. Everything below is the whole system; if a new element can't cite one of these rules, it doesn't ship.

Principles
01 · Two voicesHanken Grotesk is the words. Spline Sans Mono is the data: dates, figures, domains, labels of record. Nothing else, ever.
02 · Red is reservedRed marks status, verdicts, focus and the house bracket. It is never decoration. One red idea per region of the page.
03 · Flat, except real thingsThe record is flat. Depth is earned by things from outside the sheet: product screenshots, and overlays that float above it (the availability note, the die popover).
04 · Rules draw structureA 2px ink rule opens a section. A 1px hairline divides entries. Fills and boxes are for evidence and overlays only, never to fake hierarchy.
05 · Recompose for mobileColumns become stacked tallies; the 2×2 project grid becomes one column; gutters become inline labels. Never shrink the desktop layout.
06 · Every word is auditedMeta exists only if a visitor acts on it. No catalogue numbers, no read times, no section counts, no badges that only say the obvious.
07 · Calm over cleverNo CTAs, no stamps, no pulsing badges. Availability is a quiet line; outcomes are quiet marks. The work carries the weight.
Colour

Warm off-white with a 4% grain tile (SVG noise, baked into the body background in ledger.css). Paper tooth, not paper colour. One red does all the load-bearing accent work.

Personality

Personality lives in behaviour, real photos and honest copy, never decorative doodles. The homepage stays a calm record; the things he loves get real room on their own pages (coffee, shelf) and one warm line in the intro.

Six sides of meThe wooden die in the nav rolls to reveal one of six true lines about him. Hovering it before the first roll shows its own "roll me" invite; it never navigates. His board-games love, made literal.
One warm lineThe intro closes on coffee, board games and Simba, linking the go-to places and the shelf, so it reads as a person, not a menu of things to click.
The real catSimba is the actual photo in the hero, with a tap/hover speech bubble. No drawn cat competes with the real one.
Place & timeOne mono clock carries Bengaluru · time; the availability note reads the same clock to set an honest reply expectation. Thoughtful, not a gimmick.
Faces the lightThe paper warms and cools with Bengaluru's real sun (dawn / day / dusk / night, via data-light).
RuleIf a personality element can't be a behaviour, a real photo, or honest copy, it doesn't ship. Rich object galleries belong on dedicated pages.
Motion
Die rollTumbles ~520ms, pips redraw to the rolled face, popover springs in. Re-roll never repeats the last number; hovering before the first roll shows the invite.
TallyFigures count up over 700ms (cubic ease-out) when they enter view. Once.
AssembleRows, cards and posts snap into place on scroll (spring ease); a 3.5s failsafe guarantees nothing stays hidden.
PopoverThe availability note and the die popover fade + spring in (~160ms), close on outside-click or Esc.
Card liftProject cards and work thumbnails lift a few px with a warmer shadow on hover: the only depth the record allows.
Arrow slideIndex arrow fades in and slides 4px on hover. Always visible on touch.
DisciplineDurations ≤ 700ms. Nothing loops but the coffee steam. prefers-reduced-motion kills everything.
Never
  • A third typeface, or mono set as prose.
  • Red used decoratively, or two red ideas in one region.
  • Buttons, CTAs, or pulsing status badges: availability is a quiet line.
  • A generic pill or box to tag something: the house bracket, or nothing.
  • Meta nobody acts on (catalogue numbers, read times, "est." lines, obvious badges).
  • Depth on anything that isn't a real screenshot or a floating overlay.
  • Shrinking the desktop grid to fit a phone; a grey placeholder where a real image should be.
